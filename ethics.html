04 / Data Notice

Ethics & Data

Licensing, privacy handling, known bias, and how the demographic findings should — and shouldn't — be used.

License

Public-domain sources

Both underlying datasets — NHTSA's Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS) and the U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey (ACS) 5-Year Estimates — are U.S. government open data in the public domain. No data-sharing agreement was required to use them.

Privacy

No PII, suppression respected

FARS victim records are anonymized and indexed only by randomized case/person numbers. The enrichment pipeline actively blanks Census privacy-suppression codes (e.g. -666666666) so low-population tracts aren't de-anonymized.

Bias

Underreporting bias

Police-reported crash data undercounts minor pedestrian and cyclist incidents, skewing the sample toward severe outcomes. Findings are scoped to reported fatal crashes and are not a general measure of total risk.

Equity

Careful use of demographic findings

Tract-level demographic associations describe neighborhoods, not individual victims. A hotspot model that under-represents lower-baseline-traffic corridors could misdirect safety investment, so these results are meant to prioritize investigation — not to stigmatize communities or replace site-specific engineering review.

Scope

Descriptive, not causal

No supervised predictive model was built: the dataset contains only fatal crashes, so there is no defensible non-fatal comparison group. All results are descriptive statistical associations and spatial clusters, not causal claims.

Attribution
